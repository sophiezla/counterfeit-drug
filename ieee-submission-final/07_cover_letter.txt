Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Auditing Provenance Confounding in Image Authenticity Classification: A Counterfeit-Medicine Case Study," for publication in IEEE Access as a Research Article.

The paper addresses a dataset-construction problem that affects image authenticity classification broadly: when the inauthentic class is obtained by a different route than the authentic class, acquisition metadata predicts the label and every partition of the data inherits the association. We formalize and operationalize a pre-training provenance audit for this condition and demonstrate it on a public counterfeit-medicine dataset, where container format alone reproduces all 510 labels, so that high in-distribution accuracy cannot be read as evidence of authentication ability. Two independent external evaluations — a balanced set of regulator-confirmed falsified products against authentic-labeled photographs, and an acquisition-shift test on independently captured photographs — show the consequence, and an exploratory correction shows that removing one provenance signal can expose another. The audit is deliberately simple; the contribution is its specification and the case study that shows why it is needed.

The manuscript has not been published or presented elsewhere and is not under consideration by any other journal. All code, derived data and the manuscript sources are public at https://github.com/sophiezla/counterfeit-drug and archived at Zenodo (concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21936720; release v1.4.3, doi:10.5281/zenodo.22758850).
